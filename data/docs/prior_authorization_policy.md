# Lakeshore Health PPO: Prior Authorization Policy

Effective January 1, 2026. Lakeshore Health PPO is a fictional plan used for demonstration only.

## What Prior Authorization Means

Prior authorization (also called pre-approval or pre-certification) means the plan must approve certain services before you receive them. The plan checks that the service is medically necessary and is the right setting for your care. Prior authorization is not a guarantee of payment; the claim must still meet the plan's other terms, such as eligibility and benefits.

## Services That Require Prior Authorization

The following services need prior authorization when they are not part of an emergency:

- Advanced imaging: MRI, MRA, CT, CTA, PET scans and nuclear cardiology studies.
- Planned inpatient hospital admissions, including admissions to skilled nursing and rehabilitation facilities.
- Outpatient surgeries listed on the plan's surgical procedure list, including spine surgery, joint replacement and bariatric surgery.
- Sleep studies done in a facility.
- Durable medical equipment over $1,000, such as power wheelchairs and CPAP machines.
- Home health care and home infusion therapy.
- Inpatient mental health and substance use treatment, and partial hospitalization programs.
- Genetic testing.
- Specialty drugs on Tier 4 of the formulary (see the Pharmacy Formulary).

## Services That Do Not Require Prior Authorization

- Emergency room care, and any imaging or tests done during an emergency.
- Primary care and specialist office visits.
- Preventive care, including screening mammograms and colonoscopies.
- Standard X-rays, ultrasounds and routine lab work.
- Urgent care visits.
- Outpatient therapy for mental health.

## How to Request Prior Authorization

Your in-network provider usually submits the request for you. If you see an out-of-network provider, you are responsible for making sure the request is submitted.

1. The ordering provider submits the request through the Lakeshore Provider Portal, by fax to 1-800-555-0199, or by phone at 1-800-555-0177.
2. The request must include the diagnosis, the requested service and its procedure code, the place of service, and clinical notes that support medical necessity.
3. For imaging, include prior treatments tried (for example, physical therapy or medication) and how long symptoms have lasted.
4. Submit the request at least 5 business days before the scheduled service when possible.

## Decision Timeframes

- Standard requests: a decision within 5 business days of receiving all needed information.
- Urgent requests (when waiting could seriously harm your health): a decision within 72 hours.
- The plan notifies both you and your provider of the decision in writing. Approvals list the approved service, the number of visits or units, and the dates the approval is valid.
- Most approvals are valid for 60 days from the approval date.

## If a Request Is Denied

- The denial letter explains the reason and how to appeal.
- Your provider can ask for a peer-to-peer review with a Lakeshore medical director within 14 days of the denial.
- You or your provider can file a formal appeal within 180 days of the denial. Standard appeals are decided within 30 days; urgent appeals within 72 hours.

## What Happens If Prior Authorization Is Not Obtained

If a service needs prior authorization and none was obtained, the claim may be denied. For in-network providers, the provider cannot bill you for the denied amount. For out-of-network providers, you may be responsible for the full cost.
